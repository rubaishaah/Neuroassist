This is Sam.

Her hands no longer follow her. Her thoughts still do.

Sam41 · lives on her own · living with motor neurone disease

Three years ago Sam was diagnosed with motor neurone disease (also called ALS). It has slowly weakened her arms and hands, and her speech has become slurred and tiring. Lifting a cup, picking up her phone or finding the TV remote now depends on someone else.

Her mind is as sharp as ever, and her eyes move freely. So at home she has a robot arm beside her table and wears a light EEG cap. When she imagines moving her left or right hand, the cap picks up the change in her brain rhythms and the arm moves that way.

The trouble is that brain signals are noisy. The decoder reads Sam's intention correctly only about 4 times in 5. Every fifth step goes the wrong way, and reaching for a cup turns into a slow zig-zag.

Sam is a fictional persona, based on everyday needs that people with MND describe. The decoder accuracy comes from real EEG recordings.
