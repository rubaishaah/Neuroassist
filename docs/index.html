A robot arm that reads noisy brain signals and shares control with you

Under the hood

The decision loop, every 2 seconds

  1. Decode. Your imagined hand becomes a calibrated probability p(right) and an uncertainty in bits.
  2. Infer. A Bayesian belief over the objects fuses the decode (which side of the gripper is the goal?), your gaze (with an outlier term against stray glances) and a task-context prior. Old evidence slowly fades so you can change your mind.
  3. Share. The arm moves by α × robot direction + (1 − α) × your direction. α grows with the robot's confidence, is capped at 0.85, and drops sharply when a confident decode disagrees. That is your veto.

What is simulated here

  • Real EEG mode (default when recordings are available): each press replays a real day-2 trial of the imagined hand from the chosen BCI IV 2a participant, and the robot receives that trial's real decoder output. The decoder was trained on the same person's day-1 session.
  • Simulated mode: each decision is a draw from two equal-variance Gaussians whose separation gives the chosen accuracy, so p(right) is exactly calibrated. No EEG is shown.
  • What stays simulated in both: you choose the hand with a key press, so the brain that produced the trace is the recorded participant's, not yours.
  • Gaze is simulated, taken from your pointer, or estimated from your webcam. Webcam video never leaves your browser.
Offline evaluation on real EEG

Shared control turned a 75% system into a 98% one

The same controller was tested offline on recordings from 9 people in the BCI Competition IV 2a dataset (left/right motor imagery, decoder trained on day 1, driven by day-2 recordings), 60 runs per condition.

ConditionSuccessTimeUser control
Direct BCI75.7%36.0 s100%
Gaze autonomy99.1%28.1 s0%

Adaptive shared97.6%27.6 s24%

Adaptive shared vs direct BCI: +22.6 points, Wilcoxon p = 0.016, n = 9. Gaze-only autonomy matches it when gaze is clean, but leaves the user no control, and falls to 52% when only 30% of fixations land on the target (shared control: 83%).
