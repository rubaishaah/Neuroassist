Three days with Sam, three ways to share control

Who should steer: Sam, the robot, or both?

Each participant spends one "day" with each system, in an order that rotates between participants. The systems are called A, B and C, so nobody knows which is which.

Sam steers aloneDirect BCI: the arm follows the decoded left/right signal only.
Sam's share of steering: 100%
The robot decidesGaze and context autonomy: the robot heads for whatever Sam seems to want.
Sam's share of steering: 0%
Shared, adaptiveThe robot blends its guess with Sam's signal, takes more control when confident (up to 85%), and yields when her signal clearly disagrees.
Sam's share of steering: changes moment to moment

A day's plan

  • 6 requests: 2 morning, 2 midday, 2 evening.
  • 2 runs where Sam changes her mind halfway.
  • 2 runs where something on the table buzzes for attention.
  • The same plan every day, so the systems face identical situations.

What we measure

  • Whether the right item was fetched, and how fast.
  • How much of the steering stayed with Sam.
  • Recovery after a change of mind; grabs of the buzzing distractor.
  • After each day: workload (NASA-TLX), felt in control, trust, enjoyment.

What's real and what's simulated

  • Real: participants, their gaze, their choices, timing and ratings.
  • Simulated: the decoder. In the study it misreads 21% of presses, matching the average measured on real EEG. In free play you can replay real recorded EEG trials instead.
  • So the brain in the loop is not the participant's own. A live EEG study is the next step.

How the shared system decides

  • Decode: each press becomes a calibrated probability of "right".
  • Infer: a Bayesian belief over the objects fuses that probability, gaze (with an outlier term for stray glances) and Sam's routine.
  • Share: arm motion = α × robot direction + (1 − α) × Sam's direction.
